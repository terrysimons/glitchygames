    Zombies Ain't My Neighbors Architecture#

    Zombies Ain't My Neighbors is a late-night creature-feature run-and-gun for up to four players, on the keyboard or gamepads. Four kids at a sleepover put on their VR headsets, and the monsters of a cancelled horror show climb out of the TV and into the neighborhood. Every sprite, sound and note of music is to be written in the engine's own formats.

    It is being built feature by feature. So far: the title screen, four player slots each blinking PRESS START in its player's colour, joined from the keyboard or any of up to four pads; a TV title card, FEATURE PRESENTATION; and the neighborhood, a yard larger than the screen where each joined player walks their own kid (Dex, Marisol, Theo, June: 32x48 SNES-era sprites written by the game's own rig, zombies_aint_my_neighbors/art/) under one camera that keeps the group on screen. Four old TV sets in the yard push Lawn Zombies out through their screens every 6 to 9 seconds (3 per set, 10 in all); a zombie that touches a kid knocks it out, and the kid gets back up and blinks, untouchable, for two seconds. The kids fight back with the Channel Changer, a TV remote that fires zaps of static four times a second: one zap crumbles a zombie, and three zaps break a TV into its next stage (cracked, smashed and sparking, destroyed and smoking), each stage spawning half as often, until a destroyed one spawns nothing. Neighbors wait round the yard to be rescued: a kid's touch rescues one and a zombie's loses one. Each kid has 3 lives. When every neighbor is rescued or lost and at least one was rescued, a gate opens in the top hedge; a kid through it clears the stage, and the next stage brings back only the neighbors rescued. Every life lost, or a stage with nobody rescued, and the TV switches off: game over. Every moment of a stage has its sound, and a theremin-and-organ score plays under it. An intro opens every launch: the kids at their sleepover, the TV bursting and a Lawn Zombie climbing out; whenever the title then stands idle, unattended, it shows a demo of four kids clearing stage 1 or the intro again, in turn, as an arcade cabinet would. The design, its decisions and the engine wishlist are in the master plan, plans/2026-11-14-zombies-aint-my-neighbors.md.

    Its package: src/games/zombies-aint-my-neighbors/, a workspace member (Games as Their Own Packages).

    Running it#

    uv run zombies-aint-my-neighbors
uv run zombies-aint-my-neighbors --input-mode joystick   # read every pad on the joystick layer (see below)
uv run zombies-aint-my-neighbors --verify                # replay its golden, headless

    The game adds no options of its own; the engine's are in Argument System.

    Controls#

    Action Keyboard Gamepad
    Skip the intro, or the attract demo any key any button
    Join (a device that holds no slot) Enter or keypad Enter START
    Begin (a joined device), and skip the feature card Enter or keypad Enter START
    Back from the feature card to the title (between stages the run is kept: the next start returns to the same stage) Backspace BACK
    Walk (the neighborhood) WASD or the arrows (by position) left stick or d-pad
    Fire the Channel Changer (the neighborhood; hold to keep firing) J or right Shift (by position) A (button 0 in --input-mode joystick)
    Switch weapons: the Channel Changer or the laser pointer (the neighborhood) K (by position) Y (button 3 in --input-mode joystick)
    Shine the laser pointer (hold; it warms up for 0.4 s first) J or right Shift, holding the laser pointer A, holding the laser pointer
    Pause (every screen) P position; Enter in the neighborhood START in the neighborhood
    Resume from the pause screen Space START or A
    Quit Q or Esc (on release) B or Back on the pause screen

    In --input-mode joystick, START is read by its game-controller number on the raw joystick layer. Many pads number their buttons differently there (on Xbox-layout pads that number is often Back or View), so another button may join and START may do nothing; the engine's ActionMap documents the same caveat. Leave the input mode at auto to use the controller mapping. A per-device lookup of START's raw button is on the master plan's Engine wishlist.

    The keys are bound by position (scancodes), so they sit in the same place on every layout. The game never uses Space or Esc for its own actions: the engine's shared pause and game-over screens use them, in the browser too. J sits under a WASD hand and right Shift under an arrows hand. In the browser on a touch screen, Start and Pause press Enter and P, a D-pad on the left presses the arrows, Fire holds J and Switch presses K. The D-pad walks four ways: each button presses one key, so a diagonal takes two fingers.

    The screens#

    flowchart LR
    Launch(["launch"]) --> Title
    Title -- "first frame, at launch" --> Intro["IntroScene<br/>the sleepover, the TV bursts"]
    Title -- "20 s idle, nobody joined:<br/>the demo and the intro in turn" --> Demo["AttractScene<br/>four kids clear stage 1"]
    Title -- "20 s idle, its next turn" --> Intro
    Intro -- "8 s, or any key or button" --> Title
    Demo -- "its end, or any key or button" --> Title
    Title["TitleScene<br/>4 slots: join"] -- "joined player's start" --> Card["FeatureCardScene<br/>who is playing"]
    Card -- "2 s, or start" --> Yard["NeighborhoodScene<br/>the kids walk"]
    Card -- "back" --> Title
    Yard -- "last player unplugged" --> Title
    Card -- "last player unplugged" --> Title
    Title -- "P" --> Pause["PauseScene<br/>(engine)"]
    Pause -- "Space" --> Title

    Both screens are RosterScenes (roster_scene.py): they keep the join roster and read the same controls (one ActionMap, RosterScene._create_action_map). Each says what a start press means (_press_start) and what an unplugged pad means (_device_left). RosterScene is a PadTolerantScene (pad_tolerant.py), which deliberately ignores the pad events the screens don't use (sticks, hats, touchpads, sensors, raw device events), so --no-unhandled-events never trips on a pad.

    While the card shows, it builds the coming stage's sprites (kids, TVs, zombies, zaps, neighbors, the gate) into a sprite kit (sprite_kit.py), one sprite a frame, and hands the kit to the stage, which takes each sprite out of it instead of loading its file: the stage begins in about 2 ms instead of 0.35 s. Building only loads files; the stage still places everything and draws every random number, so a replay that starts on a stage rebuilds it exactly. A cleared stage hands the kit on to the next card, and a scene given no kit (a replay's rebuilt stage) makes its own.

    The intro and the idle loop#

    Feature plan 12 (plans/2026-11-19-zombies-aint-my-neighbors-12-intro-and-idle-loop.md). The intro (intro.py) plays one table of timed cues (intro_timeline.py): the four kids sit in their sleeping bags before the TV, the horror host on screen; at 4 s static, at 5.5 s the screen bulges, at 6 s it bursts (the kids start, the screen shakes), the monster's hands reach out, a Lawn Zombie climbs out, and at 8 s the title. Each frame acts on the cues it crossed, so a hitch fires each once, in order. Its nine sprites are drawn by art/intro_rig.py.

    • At every launch the title the engine builds (TitleScene(opening=True)) hands over to the intro on its first frame wherever the CRT power-on would play (launch_showing.py: not on the dummy driver, in a replay, recording, audit or rebaseline, or with --crt-power-on off), and every connected pad rumbles at the burst. Every other place that builds a title passes opening=False, so leaving a stage never replays it.
    • The idle loop: a title left IDLE_SECONDS (20 s) with no key, pad button or join, nobody joined and no run held, shows an attraction unattended, then returns to the title: the attract demo and the intro take turns, the demo first, since the intro has just played at launch (title.Attraction, the title's next_attraction). In the loop the intro rumbles nothing.
    • Any key or pad button skips it, P included, even on the frame it ends. The title it hands back ignores the keys and buttons held as it opens, each until it is seen up (awaiting_release), so the skipping press never joins anyone while every other key and button works at once.

    The attract demo#

    Feature plan 13 (plans/2026-11-19-zombies-aint-my-neighbors-13-attract-demo.md). The game's bundled demo, src/games/zombies-aint-my-neighbors/src/zombies_aint_my_neighbors/demos/neighborhood_four_kids.jsonl.gz, is four kids clearing stage 1 together: Dex on the keyboard, Marisol, Theo and June on stand-in pads. They zap their way round the yard, destroy two TVs and save four neighbors; one reaches the open gate and it ends halfway through the cheer, before the STAGE 2 card. The Arcade can play it on a tile like any bundled demo.

    • Playing it: AttractScene (attract.py) replays it as an inner game (glitchygames.inner_game) at its recorded 800x480 and draws the picture scaled to the screen, letterboxed when the shapes differ, under a DEMO label. It is heard; its pads are the recording's, so nothing rumbles. Any key or pad button goes back to the title (which waits for that input's release), as does the demo's end; a demo that can't play goes back with a warning. The title declares the inner game (INNER_GAMES = 1) so the engine sets its sound channels aside at startup. Building it takes about a quarter of a second on a Mac (the first showing nearly a second), during which the intro's last frame stays on screen.
    • Recording it: the demo starts in DemoNeighborhoodScene (demo_neighborhood.py), the stage with the four players already joined. scripts/demo_inputs.py's ZombiesDemoSteering steers the kids live as the recording runs, with the planner's own steering (scripts/plan_neighborhood_route.py, demo_steering): the kids move as a group (the group's screen span keeps them together) toward the neighbor nearest their middle, P4 breaks off for a nearby TV, and all head for the gate once no neighbor stands. plan_demo plays the stage the same way to set the demo's length (--demo prints it). Re-plan and re-record (scripts/record_bundled_demos.py --demo zombies_aint_my_neighbors_four_kids) when gameplay changes: test_zombies_aint_my_neighbors_demo_route.py fails until then, and checks that the shipped demo, replayed as the attract scene replays it, still shows every kid walking and zapping, a TV destroyed, a zombie zapped, neighbors saved and the clear with all four cheering.

    The title is the game's class for now, so its golden replay stays in one scene, as tests/replay/test_golden_replays.py requires. When gameplay arrives, the golden moves to the gameplay scene.

    The neighborhood#

    NeighborhoodScene (neighborhood.py) gives each joined slot its kid (kid.py), an animated world sprite. Each player's direction comes from their own device (controls.py): the keyboard's WASD or arrows, or a pad's d-pad or left stick (past the engine's axis_deadzone), read on the controller layer when the pad has one and on the joystick layer otherwise (joystick mode, which scripted sessions force). Kids walk 120 px a second, never faster diagonally, inside a 1600x960 yard, a mown checkerboard lawn; the group never spans more than the screen less 24 px a side, and the camera (no smoothing) aims at the middle of the group's bounding box, so everyone is always on screen. Kids are drawn in order of their feet. A kid faces the nearest of eight directions: down, up and left, and the diagonals down-left and up-left (three-quarter views), each drawn; right, down-right and up-right show their left-hand frames mirrored, each frame flipped once into a cache the scene owns, with hitbox_flip_x set. A pad unplugged here takes its kid with it; with nobody left, the game goes back to the title.

    Each player's fire (controls.player_firing) fires their kid's Channel Changer (feature plan 6, plans/2026-11-15-zombies-aint-my-neighbors-6-channel-changer.md): a press fires at once and holding fires every 0.25 s. Standing, the kid plays shoot_<facing>; walking, walk-shoot_<facing>; diagonals use the side pose, but the zap flies the eight-way facing. The remote (channel_changer.py) is drawn on the frame's hand point only while shooting, behind the kid when it faces up. A zap (zap.py) flies 320 px a second for 0.6 s, then fizzles; it strikes the first zombie or standing TV its hitbox meets and passes through kids. A zapped zombie flashes, crumbles and fades, freeing its TV's place under the caps at once. A TV takes 3 zaps a stage; each stage doubles its wait between spawns, and destroying one shakes the world and rumbles the destroying player's pad.

    The laser pointer (feature plan 8, plans/2026-11-15-zombies-aint-my-neighbors-8-laser-pointer.md) is a lure. K or a pad's Y switches each kid between it and the Channel Changer. Held, it warms up for 0.4 s (the kid aims, the battery already drawing), then shines a red dot 160 px ahead of the kid's feet, a beam fading in and out from the pointer to the dot; zombies within 240 px of a dot chase it instead of kids or neighbors, but still knock out whoever they walk into. The battery (laser.py) gives 3 s of shine, refills 0.3 s a second when not in use, and once flat stays dead until a third full. The heads-up display shows each player's weapon and battery. Each kid keeps its weapon into the next stage, its battery full again.

    The run (run_state.RunState: the stage, its neighbors and each player's lives) passes from scene to scene in its JSON form, as the roster does, so a replay rebuilds any stage. Neighbors (neighbor.py) stand at places drawn from the named stream zombies_aint_my_neighbors.neighbor-places, panic when a zombie is within 96 px, and play the saved or lost effect when rescued or lost; zombies chase the nearest kid or neighbor. A knockout on a player's last life leaves the kid lying, fading for 1 s, then gone for the stage (the camera and the group's span then leave it out). The exit door (exit_door.py) opens when no neighbor is left and one was rescued; a kid through it clears the stage, the kids cheer for 1.5 s, and the feature card shows STAGE n before the next. The game over (game_over.py) is the picture collapsing to a line, then a dot, then GAME OVER, and the title after 4 s or a start. The heads-up display (hud.py) shows each player's lives along the top and the stage and the neighbors rescued along the bottom.

    Zombies and zaps come from pools the scene builds before play (pool.py), so no sprite file loads mid-game: the engine keeps no cache of loaded sprite files, and a zombie's took 27 to 41 ms to load. Idle ones are out of every group; a spawn or a shot takes one, and one that has gone is given back. A test fails if any sprite file loads during twenty busy seconds.

    Built from options alone, as a recording's first scene is, the neighborhood has the keyboard as P1. The game has two golden replays: the title's (golden.jsonl.gz) and the neighborhood's (golden-neighborhood.jsonl.gz: walking every way, then firing with J and right Shift, a zombie zapped and the top-left TV destroyed); main (launcher.py) starts a replay on the scene its recording names, as Brave Adventurer's launcher does.

    Sounds#

    Every sound is an effect in one sound file, sounds/sounds.toml, written by sound_rig.py. Do not edit the file by hand: change the rig and run uv run python -m zombies_aint_my_neighbors.sound_rig. The sounds are declared in SOUNDS on RosterScene, so the engine compiles them while the game starts.

    • The remote's zap: warbly theremin radio waves. Six swoops of a sine between about 600 and 700 Hz, 0.22 s (under the 0.25 s firing rhythm), with a faint hiss.
    • The laser: a rising "wheeee" through the 0.4 s warm-up, then a high wobble looping while the dot shines, panning with the kid, and fading out when the dot goes out.

    • The zombies: three low moans. A zombie that finds a new target (a kid, a neighbor or a laser dot) groans one time in three, never within 3 s of its own last groan and never within half a second of any groan, so a crowd does not moan in chorus. The rolls come from the named stream zombies_aint_my_neighbors.zombie-groans, so a replay repeats them.

    • The TVs: a picture-tube thump and a burst of static as a zombie climbs out; a zap plays only the strongest of what it did: a glassy tick (a hit), a crunch of glass (a break into the next stage), or the crunch with a low boom and a fizzle (destroyed).

    • The neighbors: three bright notes chirping up when one is rescued; a falling wail and a thud when one is lost.
    • A knockout: a cartoon bonk, once per knockout.
    • The gate: an iron creak as it begins to swing open.
    • The game over: the tube's whine falling away as the picture collapses, and a click.

    The engine has no vibrato in a baked sound yet (milestone M4), so each warble is a chain of swoops, each lasting a whole number of its own cycles so the chain is one unbroken wave. Each sound pans by its source's place across the screen (-0.6 at the left edge, 0.6 at the right).

    The score#

    The music (music.py) is played on catalog instruments: a theremin, a theatre organ in two voices, a pizzicato double bass and timpani. A cue is layers played together, each one instrument playing one melody, every layer exactly as long as its cue.

    • The stage loop (music.STAGE): D minor, 120 beats a minute, eight bars (16 s), from the stage's first frame.
    • The stage clear (music.STAGE_CLEAR): the loop fades out over 0.3 s and the band lands on D major for the 1.5 s cheer.
    • The game over (music.GAME_OVER): the organ's dun, dun, DUNNN as GAME OVER shows.

    Each cue is one sound, an engine mix (music.cue_mix): its layers rendered and added at their volumes, cached as every sound is, so it is built once and read from the disk cache on later launches. The stage loop is a looping mix, exactly 16.000 s with every tail folded onto its start, played once with loop=True: the mixer repeats it sample for sample, on one channel. Every cue plays on the music bus at priority 80, so a storm of zaps never steals it.

    The score's mixes (music.SCORE) are declared with the effects in SOUNDS, so the engine builds them while the game starts, during the CRT power-on; the stage and the game over then find them built. Measured 2026-11-18 on the development Mac: the stage loop builds in 188 ms (on the first launch, holding back one frame of the power-on; later launches read it from the disk cache, and where the power-on is skipped it is built before the first scene), the stings in 21 and 43 ms; the score holds about 15 MB (9.9 MB of it the loop: float and int16 samples and the mixer's stereo copy), and its loudest peak is 0.79, so nothing clips.

    The join roster#

    JoinRoster (roster.py) is a frozen pydantic model: four slots, each free or held by one device, the keyboard or a pad by its SDL instance id. join puts a device in the lowest free slot; a device never holds two slots, and a fifth device is ignored; leave frees a slot. The title and the card pass the roster between them as a constructor argument, and give it to a replay as JSON (replay_arguments).

    Pads already plugged in when the game starts are never announced to a scene (the engine synchronises them before any scene exists), so joining reads only the press's instance id.

    The engine tells only the active scene that a device left, so a pad unplugged on the pause screen is caught when the title or the card resumes: setting a screen up again drops every joined pad the engine no longer sees (RosterScene.setup).

    Drawing#

    Every on-screen thing is a sprite (TextPicture, text.py). Every image, the title, each slot in each state and each player label, is rendered once in setup(); a frame only picks which image a slot shows and whether it is shown, so no frame renders text. Free slots blink together, half a second shown and half hidden, from Scene.game_time, so a replay blinks exactly as the recording did.

    Player Colour
    P1 ghoul green (92, 230, 92)
    P2 pumpkin orange (255, 140, 26)
    P3 séance violet (176, 97, 255)
    P4 TV-glow cyan (51, 214, 255)

    The text uses the bundled Bitstream Vera Bold for now; the sprite feature, which starts with an audited character BOM, may replace it.

    Tests#

    The member's tests (src/games/zombies-aint-my-neighbors/tests/) run on the real display and check pixels. Pad tests use the engine's stand-in pad (tests/input/stand_in_devices.py) through the real devices door, plugged in and out mid-run. Its golden replay (tests/fixtures/replays/golden.jsonl.gz) joins the keyboard after one blink cycle, pauses and resumes, opens the feature card with keypad Enter and comes back with Enter, then quits.
