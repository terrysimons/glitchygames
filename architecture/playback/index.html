    Gameplay Playback#

    Playback is how GlitchyGames records a play session and runs it again, through the real engine, frame for frame. It is what makes a bug report reproducible, a golden replay a regression test, a demo or an instant replay possible, and a performance measurement repeatable: the same recording, replayed on the same engine, draws the same sprites in every frame (src/glitchygames/replay/driver.py, whose docstring is the replay's contract).

    These pages explain how it works. For every flag, option and exit code, the command reference is Gameplay Recording and Replay; these pages link into it rather than repeat it.

    The idea in one picture#

    Everything the engine takes in from the outside world (events, frame time, polled input, devices, saves, the clocks and the random seed) passes through one set of doors (src/glitchygames/sources.py, EngineSources). Live play installs the live doors; a recording wraps them and writes down what passes through; a replay installs doors that serve the recording instead. Everything downstream of the doors, the game included, runs unchanged.

    flowchart LR
    world["Outside world<br/>(SDL queue, keyboard, pads,<br/>save files, clocks)"]
    live["LiveSources<br/>src/glitchygames/live_sources.py"]
    recording["RecordingSources<br/>src/glitchygames/recording.py"]
    file[("recording<br/>.jsonl.gz")]
    replay["ReplaySources<br/>src/glitchygames/replay/driver.py"]
    engine["SceneManager + the game<br/>(unchanged in every mode)"]

    world --> live
    live --> engine
    live -. "--record-trace wraps" .-> recording
    recording --> engine
    recording --> file
    file --> replay
    replay --> engine

    The modes at a glance#

    Mode How it starts Doors Paced? Compares? Writes?
    Live play <game> LiveSources yes, to the target FPS no nothing
    Record <game> --record-trace PATH RecordingSources around LiveSources yes no the recording
    Headless replay --replay-trace PATH --headless ReplaySources no (paced_frame_seconds is 0.0) every frame, stops at the first difference nothing
    Watched replay --replay-trace PATH ReplaySources, driven by the viewer by the viewer's 60 Hz outer tick every frame, pauses at a difference a bookmarked copy, if asked
    Live play against OLD <game> --compare-trace OLD the live host's doors, plus a headless child replaying OLD yes no (the onion skin only draws) nothing (or --record-trace)
    Rebaseline --replay-trace OLD --rebaseline NEW --headless ReplaySources, with a recorder over them no counts every differing frame, never stops NEW
    Measure glitchygames measure a rebaseline per recording, in a child no as a rebaseline ledger rows
    Verify glitchygames verify a headless replay per recording, in a child no as a headless replay nothing
    Audit --replay-trace PATH --audit two headless replays, in fresh processes no the two runs' frame digests nothing

    Sources: the pacing column is paced_frame_seconds in src/glitchygames/sources.py and rule 13 of src/glitchygames/replay/driver.py; the watched replay's tick is CONTROL_TICKS_PER_SECOND in src/glitchygames/replay/controls.py; live play against OLD is rule 9 of src/glitchygames/replay/tools/compare.py; the rebaseline is src/glitchygames/replay/rebaseline.py; measure and verify are src/glitchygames/replay/tools/measure.py and verify.py; the audit is src/glitchygames/replay/audit.py.

    Which page answers what#

    1. How a frame runs: the steps of one frame in SceneManager's order, where each door is read, and where a replay's sources take over from the live ones.
    2. Speeds and timing: live pacing, headless and watched replays, the catch-up rule, --replay-fps real, live play against OLD and step back. A frame's logic is the same at every speed; only the waiting differs.
    3. The recording file: the .jsonl.gz layout, every field and why it is kept, what is deliberately not kept, and what is stored versus derived.
    4. How we test playback: golden replays, golden traces, the replay-tool tests on stand-in games, verify, measure and rebaseline, the determinism audit, the doors guard and the memory guard.
