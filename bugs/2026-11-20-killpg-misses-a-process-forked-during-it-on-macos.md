# A Process Group Killed as It Forks Kept a Member Running on macOS

- **Description**: Under a loaded `uv run pytest --dummy-only` on the M4 Max (2026-11-20, worktree `glitchygames-arcade-5-playback-core`; recorded in that branch's final-fix report), `tests/test_ansible_ai_service.py::test_a_run_after_one_that_was_killed_proceeds_at_once` raised `subprocess.TimeoutExpired` ("timed out after 10 s"), the child-process guard errored for the one process it left (and killed it), and the next test on the same xdist worker (`test_the_libstdcxx_floor_is_the_newest_gcc_either_version_needs[3.4.33 1.3.15-14.1.0]`) failed on two `ResourceWarning: unclosed file <_io.TextIOWrapper name=7>`, the timed-out test's pipes. The file alone passed (298 passed).
- **Status**: Fixed (2026-11-20, branch `fix/killpg-misses-forked-process`)
- **Priority**: Medium (a flaky suite failure on macOS; `stop_command` could leave a tool's or game's process running)
- **Root Cause**: On macOS, `os.killpg(SIGKILL)` can miss a process the group forks while the signal is delivered: the leader dies and the new process runs on in the group, holding the leader's pipes. The lock tests start setup.sh in a session of its own, wait until the fake curl has logged its call, then kill the group, so the kill lands exactly when the fake curl's shell forks `sleep`. The `sleep` survived and held the pipes, `communicate(timeout=10)` timed out, and the test's `finally` left the pipes unclosed for the garbage collector to report in a later test. Reproduced with the lock tests' single fork (a one-off triage loop): 20 of 300 trials on this Mac unloaded, 4 of 300 later with lint running, 0 of 300 in a Linux container (Linux delivers a group signal to a process forked during it). That window is narrow and moves with timing, so `scripts/reproduce_killpg_fork_race.py` widens it (the shell forks 200 sleeps): `killpg` mode 20 of 50 trials. Its shell logged before its first fork until 2026-11-23, so a stop could land with no member and a trial passed vacuously (4 of 100 on macOS; #287's macOS job 5176 failed on one): it now logs after its first fork and refuses a trial whose logged member is not running, and `killpg` mode left a member in 16 of 100 trials (other tests running).
- **Test gap**: (a) nothing tested a group signal that misses a member; (b) the lock tests' cleanup was not exception-safe (no `with Popen`), so a timeout left the pipes open.
- **Defect class (property)**: after stopping a process group, no process is left in it, and what is judged left is what still runs (a process killed moments before is neither a survivor nor a reason to fail). Siblings: `glitchygames.process.stop_command` (uncovered: it signalled the group, waited for the leader alone and returned once the leader was gone; callers `replay/tools/compare.py`, `tests/driver_comparison.py`, `scripts/ai_model_comparison.py`, `scripts/mirror_measurements/live_stream_cost.py`, `scripts/reproduce_orphaned_test_processes.py`); the three hand-written `killpg` blocks in `tests/test_ansible_ai_service.py` (uncovered); `tests/child_process_guard.py` (the last line: catches survivors in tests only; uncovered: its listing counted zombies, killed and not yet reaped, as leftovers); `scripts/reproduce_orphaned_test_processes.py`'s `end_the_run` (uncovered: a hand-written SIGKILL after `stop_command`, for a run whose controller had already ended, returned before the killed workers were reaped); the check-then-kill cleanup in `tests/test_child_process_guard.py` (CI job 4416, Linux: `ProcessLookupError` at its `os.kill`, a grandchild the check saw reaped by init before the kill) and `tests/test_driver_comparison.py` (uncovered, the same pattern); `tests/replay/tools/test_command_crash_boundary.py` (covered: it already ignored `ProcessLookupError`).
- **Solution**: `stop_command` keeps the property on POSIX: after the leader is reaped, `_empty_the_group` looks at the group (`killpg(pgid, 0)`); a member left after a gentle stop gets the grace to end on its own, then the group is killed at every look until `killpg` reports it gone (ESRCH); after a SIGKILL stop the group is killed at once. macOS answers EPERM while every member left is ending: looked at again. A group still there a grace after the kill raises `ProcessGroupSurvivedError` naming the group and each member left (pid and command line, from `/bin/ps`; the per-line lint suppression the owner approved, 2026-11-20). A child that had already ended is no longer only reaped: what it left in its group gets the stop's first signal (none had been sent), then the group is emptied the same way; `end_the_run` drops its hand-written kill. The guard lists each process's state (`stat=`) and never counts a zombie as a leftover; `_empty_the_group` does not raise for a group left with only zombies (their parents outside the group have not reaped them), by the same rule (`glitchygames.process.is_zombie_state`, which the guard imports). Test cleanups kill their own child through its `Popen` (its pid cannot be reused before it is reaped), and any other process by pid through `tests/child_process_guard.kill_if_running`, which never checks first and treats a process already gone as stopped. Left as is, and documented: a pid that is not ours can be reused between its reap and that kill, and the group id between its last member's exit and the next look (both need a pid wrap within that window). The bounds count looks, so the wait ends whatever a clock does. The lock tests open setup.sh with `with subprocess.Popen(...)` (pipes always closed) and stop it with `stop_command(first_signal=SIGKILL)`. Windows is unchanged (no process groups there). Cost: a stop whose group is gone costs one extra `killpg` (ESRCH); a stop slows only when a member is left: up to the grace after a gentle stop, a few looks after a kill.
- **Changed contract**: `stop_command` on a child that had already ended now signals and empties its group on POSIX (one `killpg`, answered ESRCH, when the group is gone); before, it returned. Windows unchanged. Since 2026-11-22 (the owner's choice), `stop_command` also closes the child's pipes once it is reaped, however it returns or raises: no caller leaks one (Chrome's standard error in `scripts/measure_browser_frames.py` did), and a caller can no longer read output the child left unread after the stop (none did; all other callers write to a file). A child still running when it raises keeps its pipes. Swept the same day for every child started with a pipe: `run_command`'s launches close theirs (`with child:`), `scripts/measure_on_raspberry_pi.py` and the Ansible setup tests use `with Popen`, the other callers pass files; `scripts/ai_behaviour/paired.py`'s worker was not covered (its close skipped the stdout close and left the worker running when its wait timed out), fixed on its own branch, `fix/paired-worker-close`. Tests: `test_stop_command_closes_the_childs_pipes_once_it_is_reaped` (a running child and one that ended first, both failing before) and `test_a_child_still_running_when_the_stop_raises_keeps_its_pipes`.
