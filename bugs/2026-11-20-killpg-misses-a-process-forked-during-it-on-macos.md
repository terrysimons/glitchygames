# A Process Group Killed as It Forks Kept a Member Running on macOS

- **Description**: Under a loaded `uv run pytest --dummy-only` on the M4 Max (2026-11-20, worktree `glitchygames-arcade-5-playback-core`; recorded in that branch's final-fix report), `tests/test_ansible_ai_service.py::test_a_run_after_one_that_was_killed_proceeds_at_once` raised `subprocess.TimeoutExpired` ("timed out after 10 s"), the child-process guard errored for the one process it left (and killed it), and the next test on the same xdist worker (`test_the_libstdcxx_floor_is_the_newest_gcc_either_version_needs[3.4.33 1.3.15-14.1.0]`) failed on two `ResourceWarning: unclosed file <_io.TextIOWrapper name=7>`, the timed-out test's pipes. The file alone passed (298 passed).
- **Status**: Fixed (2026-11-20, branch `fix/killpg-misses-forked-process`)
- **Priority**: Medium (a flaky suite failure on macOS; `stop_command` could leave a tool's or game's process running)
- **Root Cause**: On macOS, `os.killpg(SIGKILL)` can miss a process the group forks while the signal is delivered: the leader dies and the new process runs on in the group, holding the leader's pipes. The lock tests start setup.sh in a session of its own, wait until the fake curl has logged its call, then kill the group, so the kill lands exactly when the fake curl's shell forks `sleep`. The `sleep` survived and held the pipes, `communicate(timeout=10)` timed out, and the test's `finally` left the pipes unclosed for the garbage collector to report in a later test. Reproduced with the lock tests' single fork (a one-off triage loop): 20 of 300 trials on this Mac unloaded, 4 of 300 later with lint running, 0 of 300 in a Linux container (Linux delivers a group signal to a process forked during it). That window is narrow and moves with timing, so `scripts/reproduce_killpg_fork_race.py` widens it (the shell forks 200 sleeps): `killpg` mode 20 of 50 trials.
- **Test gap**: (a) nothing tested a group signal that misses a member; (b) the lock tests' cleanup was not exception-safe (no `with Popen`), so a timeout left the pipes open.
- **Defect class (property)**: after stopping a process group, no process is left in it. Siblings: `glitchygames.process.stop_command` (uncovered: it signalled the group, waited for the leader alone and returned once the leader was gone; callers `replay/tools/compare.py`, `tests/driver_comparison.py`, `scripts/ai_model_comparison.py`, `scripts/mirror_measurements/live_stream_cost.py`, `scripts/reproduce_orphaned_test_processes.py`); the three hand-written `killpg` blocks in `tests/test_ansible_ai_service.py` (uncovered); `tests/child_process_guard.py` (the last line: catches survivors in tests only).
- **Solution**: `stop_command` keeps the property on POSIX: after the leader is reaped, `_empty_the_group` looks at the group (`killpg(pgid, 0)`); a member left after a gentle stop gets the grace to end on its own, then the group is killed at every look until `killpg` reports it gone (ESRCH); after a SIGKILL stop the group is killed at once. macOS answers EPERM while every member left is ending: looked at again. A group still there a grace after the kill raises `ProcessGroupSurvivedError` naming the group. The bounds count looks, so the wait ends whatever a clock does. The lock tests open setup.sh with `with subprocess.Popen(...)` (pipes always closed) and stop it with `stop_command(first_signal=SIGKILL)`. Windows is unchanged (no process groups there). Cost: a stop whose group is gone costs one extra `killpg` (ESRCH); a stop slows only when a member is left: up to the grace after a gentle stop, a few looks after a kill.
- **Not changed**: a child that had already ended before `stop_command` is still only reaped, its group not looked at (as before); the error names the group but does not list its members (listing them with `ps` needs a new lint suppression, which waits for the owner).
- **Verified**: `test_a_member_the_group_signal_missed_is_stopped_too` (both first signals) failed before the fix (`DID NOT RAISE ProcessLookupError`: the group still had its member, and the guard errored on it) and passes after; the stand-in tests cover a member given the grace then killed, a group that outlives the kill (the error), and EPERM looks; the reproduction's `stop-command` mode leaves nothing in 50 trials where its `killpg` mode left a member in 20; the three lock tests passed 10 runs in a row.
