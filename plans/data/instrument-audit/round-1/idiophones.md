## Notes

- Missing table was cleaned: array mbira and ching/chappa are aliases only. Instrument "rock gong" appears in Missing and the alias row for rock gong variants points to the sekkin/lithophone entry; the owner may merge them.
- Not-stated acoustic data: most range and partial figures are from general acoustics knowledge, not the cited pages; only angklung (G2-C6), musical saw (about 2 octaves), tingsha (clear, high, long ring), waterphone (inharmonic) and jaw harp (harmonic series by mouth) were stated by sources.
- Hard to model with at most 4 sources (oscillators/noise + envelope + filter): bianzhong (dual-pitch bells need two inharmonic modal sets), wind chimes / bell tree / mark tree / sleigh bells (many independent random strikes), rainstick and ratchet (stochastic pulse trains, need a rate envelope), musical saw / daxophone / nail violin / waterphone (friction, continuous glide, needs vibrato plus slowly varying filters), thunder sheet (long noise-driven modal build), jaw harp (moving formant over fixed buzzy fundamental; feasible with a sawtooth into a swept bandpass), flexatone (wobble needs pitch LFO), daluo/xiaoluo (pitch glide in either direction), tuning fork (trivial sine plus short high partial), verrophone/glass harmonica (sine with slow attack).
- Uncertain: "ranat" bar materials, which bars map to gender vs xylophone, and whether kemanak counts as struck or plucked; MIMO/Grove/museum sites not consulted; Korean idiophones, Southeast Asian khong wong, Burmese kyi waing, Thai khong, Philippine kulintang sets, Vietnamese dan da/dan t'rung (bamboo xylophone), Tibetan/Mongolian items, Latin American quijada/jawbone, marimba de chonta, European folk (wooden "klepetec", cuckoo clapper), Arabic naqus, middle-eastern sagat were not read and remain unaudited. Jawbone (Peru, PL) and quijada: dry rattling teeth; would be a candidate (unpitched, buzzy rattle) but alias to shaker/guiro is acceptable.
- Possible bug noted in catalog (not a code change): "tabla dayan" is in tuned percussion while "tabla bayan" is in drums and percussion, and "timpani" and "berimbau" sit in other families; these are family inconsistencies worth a look.
- Several lamellophone and gong-class items already map cleanly; the catalog already covers the main idiophone cores (glockenspiel, crotales, xylophone, marimba, vibraphone, balafon, bells, steel pans, handpan, kalimba/mbira, music box, gamelan saron/gender/bonang, singing bowl, glass harmonica, cajon, triangle, cowbell, agogo, finger cymbals, claves, woodblocks, castanets, shakers, guiro, vibraslap, whip).
