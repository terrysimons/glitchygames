# Electrophones and oddities audit

Method: every row below cites a page that was actually fetched in this session. Where a source did not state a range or timbre, the cell says "not stated" instead of guessing; the "how it sounds" column then carries only what the source supports. H-S = Hornbostel-Sachs number; only given when I am confident (class 5 = electrophones with the subdivisions 51 action, 52 amplification, 53 radioelectric per the Wikipedia H-S list), otherwise "unknown".

Source key (all fetched):
- WP-EL = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_electronic_musical_instruments and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_musical_instrument
- WP-EX = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_musical_instrument
- 120Y = https://120years.net/ (menu page; per-instrument URLs listed there)
- ODD = http://www.oddmusic.com/gallery/
- PARTCH = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_instruments_by_Harry_Partch and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Partch

## Notes

Uncertainties
- H-S numbers marked "unverified" are my recollection of the class, not read from a source; treat as hints only. I verified H-S 5, 51, 52, 53 only via the Wikipedia electronic list.
- Several Missing rows (marked "not stated" or "per ODD") are taken from one-line descriptions on the Oddmusic gallery or the Wikipedia experimental list; I did not open their individual pages. They are real but their sound is under-documented. If priority is limited, treat the ODD-only rows as low priority.
- Some rows are grouped or duplicated for convenience (e.g. kithara partch vs lyre alias). Section D includes ~30 Partch/ODD rows that are novelty items an engine owner may reasonably decline.
- Year conflicts: Stroh violin 1889 (infobox) vs 1899 (text, patent); Clavivox 1952 (120Y) vs 1956 (Wikipedia list); Cahill telharmonium 1896 development, 1897 patent; Electronde 1927 vs 1929 depending on 120Y slug.
- Failed fetches (no data): NES APU page and Game Boy sound hardware page (404); Wikipedia "List of experimental musical instruments" (404); Tubulum page (404, so tubulum is NOT included). I did not fetch pages for marxophone-like Fender-era instruments, Eigenharp timbre, nyckelharpa-likes, daxophone sound beyond the article, pikasso detail, Hydraulophone timbre beyond the article. NES info came from the Ricoh 2A03 page; Game Boy from the Chiptune page.
- Pitch ranges that the source did not state are marked "not stated". I did not invent them.

Instruments the engine may model poorly (at most 4 oscillator/noise sources with envelopes and filters)
- Sample/tape based: mellotron, chamberlin, optigan, fairlight, DPCM, Linn LM-1. Approximate only (detuned oscillators plus slow wow LFO plus filter).
- Friction/self-excited: musical saw, daxophone, cristal baschet, long string instrument, waterphone (bowed inharmonic), glass-style. Use theremin/sine plus slow attack; inharmonic partials are expensive.
- Physical/environmental: aeolian harp, sea organ, pyrophone, hydraulophone, stalacpipe, intonarumori, marble machine. Fixed pitch sets are fine; stochastic behaviour is not modelled.
- Controllers with no sound of their own: laser harp, eigenharp, continuum (EaganMatrix aside), reactable, alphasphere. Recommend not adding, or alias to a lead patch.
- Body/noise circuits: cracklebox, dermatron, atari punk console, dub siren, electroencephalophone. Noise plus erratic filter only.
- FM chips (YM2612): needs at least 2-operator FM; with 4 sources an approximation using two oscillators and a carrier/modulator is possible; 4 operators per voice exceed the source budget.
- Vocoder and talk box: need a formant filter bank to match; the existing vowel voices could be reused with a sawtooth carrier.
- Very low register: octobass C0, marimba eroica 22 Hz, blaster beam below 50 Hz are below normal speaker output; consider emphasis on overtones.
- Microtonal Partch instruments need a tuning table; the catalog currently has none.
- Possible bug/observation: the catalog's "chip pulse 25" and a "chip pulse 75" are acoustically identical, so only one is needed.
