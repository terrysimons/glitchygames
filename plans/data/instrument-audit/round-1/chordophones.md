- Not reached: MIMO, Met, MIM, Horniman and regional museum lists were not fetched. Obscure instruments likely still missing from this audit: dolceola (hammered zither, USA), zither-banjo, harp-zither/harpolyre families, Chinese qin relatives (se, zhu, yazheng), Kazakh kobyz, Mongolian tobshuur/yatga, Tuvan doshpuluur, Philippine kudyapi, Thai phin/krachappi, Bornean sape, Sardinian/Corsican cetera, kokyu relatives, tsymbaly, Hawaiian steel (lap steel is in catalog), Polynesian ukulele variants, rare keyboard strings (claviorgan, clavicytherium, giraffe piano, harp-piano, claviharp), and electric chordophones (Rickenbacker 12, Stratocaster-specific tones, Chapman stick pickup).
- Marxophone and celestaphone can be modeled as dulcimer-like decaying pluck plus repeated retrigger of the same note (hammer bounce) at ~15-25 Hz; a distinct "tremolo-on-hold" envelope is needed.
- Engine-modeling difficulty (max 4 oscillator/noise sources with envelopes and filters): sympathetic-string instruments (sitar, sarod, rabab, baryton, bazantar, dilruba, esraj, hardanger-style) need detuned partial sources plus a slow-decay noise/formant; jawari or sawari buzzing bridges (sitar, tanpura, veena, biwa, begena, nyatiti, tromba marina) need a nonlinear buzz or band-limited noise layer on the attack; aeolian harp has no attack at all and relies on slow amplitude modulation of partials; ukelin/bowed psaltery require bowed (sawtooth plus noise) sources without vibrato; tapped or two-hand instruments (chapman stick, warr, harpejji) are just plucked tones at the instrument level; guqin harmonics and sliding glissandi need a pitch-glide control rather than a static note.
