# Harps audit (family: harps), revision 2

Sources actually fetched or searched this pass (21 calls): Wikipedia articles Harp, Pedal harp, Celtic harp, Paraguayan harp, Arpa llanera, Saung, Adungu, Ennanga, Konghou, Chang, Seperewa, Gravikord, Kora (instrument), Aeolian harp, Harp guitar; web searches covering the Met collection, MIMO/Hornbostel-Sachs vocabulary and museum records (Brooklyn, Pitt Rivers, Tervuren), and Grove. Wikipedia "Bolon_(instrument)" 404'd and the "List of harps" page does not exist. Grove and MIMO returned no usable entries for harps (Grove is paywalled; MIMO only returned an unrelated H-S vocabulary page). Anything marked "(memory)" is not source-backed. Ranges are VERIFIED only where a URL is given; otherwise ESTIMATE (memory), which means unverified.

## Summary
- New missing instruments: 19 (cut from 40; duplicates, aliases and sloppy rows removed).
- Proposed entries rejected or moved: 6 (swarmandal, sasando, krar, begena, nyatiti, celestial harp held back as unconfirmed).
- Aliases: 12.
- Range results: 4 VERIFIED (concert harp, saung, gravikord, kora), 1 catalog range corrected (kora), the rest UNVERIFIED.
- Aeolian harp: the Wikipedia article calls it a wind-driven string instrument (H-S string class); the catalog has no wind-driven category, so it is added here but could instead go under "electrophones_and_oddities".

## Rejected or corrected proposed entries
- swarmandal: board zither, not a harp; move to zithers. (memory; no source fetched)
- sasando: tube zither with palm-leaf resonator; move to zithers. (memory; no source fetched)
- krar, begena, nyatiti: lyres, not harps; the catalog already has a lyre entry in this family, so add them as lyre-type entries (or move to a lyres list). (memory)
- celestial harp: only source is the oddmusic gallery; no range, no confirmation it is a distinct documented instrument. Not added.
- seperewa: keep. Corrected: source gives 6 to 14 strings (modern 10 to 14), square wooden box resonator (unlike calabash kora), thumbs-played; no range given. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seperewa
- ngombi: keep (listed above, source in Adungu article).
- gravikord: keep, corrected H-S/class as a kora-like bridge harp with 24 strings. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravikord
- konghou: keep. Ancient and modern forms exist; the modern instrument has two rows of strings with levers. No range found. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konghou
- Dropped from my own earlier draft: welsh harp, telyn, arpa celta, arpa central, kora double harp (duplicate), double harp / arpa doppia (merge with cross strung or baroque triple), hook harp, dital harp, electric harp, queen mary harp (a small wire-strung Scottish clarsach, so covered by wire strung harp), bray harp (a feature, not an instrument), kinnor (disputed lyre or harp), harp piano (belongs in keyboards or plucked strings), bolon, vajra, yazh, kafir harp, padhar harp, domu (alias of kundi), kamale and donso duplicates. The Wikipedia Harp article does name yaal/yazh, vajra, kafir harp and padhar harp, so these are genuine; they were dropped only for lack of range data. Re-add if the owner wants lower-confidence entries: yazh, vajra, kafir harp, padhar harp, bolon.

## Range checks
- concert harp: VERIFIED. 47 strings, C-flat 1 to G-flat 7 (about 6.5 octaves); some sources say C1 to G7. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_harp
- kora: CORRECTED/VERIFIED against museum entry D2 to F5 for the 21-string kora; Wikipedia gives no text range. https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/632
- saung gauk: VERIFIED C3 to F5 (13 strings). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saung
- gravikord: VERIFIED. Left side G1 to E5, right side C2 to E5, standard 24 strings, tuned G major/E minor. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravikord
- celtic harp: partially VERIFIED. Lever harps span roughly two to six octaves. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_harp
- paraguayan harp: strings 32-46, 4+ to nearly 8 octaves (no note names). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_harp
- seperewa: no range in source; UNVERIFIED.
- konghou, ngombi, adungu, ennanga, ardin, kundi: no range in any source found; UNVERIFIED.
- swarmandal C3-C6 and sasando C3-C6: UNVERIFIED and moved out.

## Gaps you could not close
- MIMO and Grove gave no direct harp listings (MIMO returned only an unrelated H-S vocabulary page; Grove is paywalled). Met and Brooklyn records confirm Egyptian arched, Fang/Gabon ngombi and Zande kundi exist but give no ranges.
- Tervuren/RMCA says north-east Congo alone has ten distinct harp types; Zande, Mangbetu and Ngbaka harps are hard to tell apart. A specific list was not found.
- Pokot harp, Bagirmi, Kotoko galdyama trio (galyma, kolo; named in the Adungu article), Tibetan and Ottoman harps (cenk) not confirmed.
- Harp-guitar variants (Dyer Style 8, Gibson Style U, Knutsen, contraguitar, floating-string models) are real (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harp_guitar) but are sub-types of the catalog entry; no ranges given.
- Pitch ranges for all African arched harps need an ethnomusicology source (e.g. the Cambridge "Human migration and African harps" paper, abstract only retrieved).
