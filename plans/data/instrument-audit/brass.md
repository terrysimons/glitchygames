## Rejected or corrected proposed entries

- hosaphone: REJECT. No source found; not in Wikipedia brass category or Met results.
- buccin: CORRECTED range to E2-Bb4 (VERIFIED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccin). Proposed Bb2-Bb4 was wrong. Note the article warns of a second "buccin" modeled on the Roman buccina that plays only four notes.
- carnyx: range C3-C5 is UNSUPPORTED. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnyx says replica resonances are inharmonic and gives no range; closest modern analog is a harmonic alto saxhorn. Needs an inharmonic partial table to be modeled faithfully.
- dungchen: "C1-C2" UNSUPPORTED; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungchen gives no pitch. Modeling: very low drone.
- karnay: ACCEPT, but G2-G3 UNSUPPORTED; page gives no range. Two forms exist (muiuz kernei goat horn 30-40 cm; jez kernei metal 1-2 m), the proposed "very long trumpet" is only the latter. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnay
- slide trumpet: ACCEPT with caution; scholars debate whether Renaissance examples existed, page gives no pitch, single slide joint. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_trumpet
- didjeribone: exists (Charlie McMahon, plastic tubing slide didgeridoo). Range unsourced. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didgeridoo
- bazooka: Wikipedia has a page "Bazooka (instrument)" in the brass category, so it exists; range unsourced. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Brass_instruments
- subcontrabass tuba: exists (category page); range unsourced.
- natural trumpet: range corrected in concept: "commonly used range from 3rd to 16th harmonic" (C instrument: roughly C3 to C6 sounding is plausible, VERIFIED by harmonic count only). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_trumpet
- keyed bugle: "about two octaves, mainly Bb with C crook" (VERIFIED); G3-C6 is wider than two octaves, so proposed range is likely too wide. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyed_bugle
- fluba: no source apart from oddmusic; UNVERIFIED.
- cornu, trutruca: existence confirmed in category listings; ranges unsourced.

## Modeling notes

- Carnyx: inharmonic resonances (VERIFIED) are not reproducible with simple harmonic oscillators; use detuned partials.
- Cornetts, serpent, ophicleide, keyed bugle: finger-hole/keyed conical bores; the engine can only approximate with filtered saw plus noise.
- Slide instruments (sackbut, slide trumpet, didjeribone, valve-slide hybrids): continuous pitch glide needed.
- Very long horns (dungchen, karnay, nafir, kakaki): low drone with few harmonics.
- Didgeridoo-style: vocal drone with formant sweeps, no FM available.
