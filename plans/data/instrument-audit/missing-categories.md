# Missing instrument categories (adversarial family audit)

Method: fetched/searched the sources below (H-S numbering, MIMO revision, Natya Shastra, bayin, per-instrument Wikipedia pages). Anything not fetched is marked UNVERIFIED. Counts are estimates (memory-informed) and should be re-audited by the per-family agents.

Sources actually read:
- H-S scheme (3-4 digit numbering, 2011 MIMO note): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbostel%E2%80%93Sachs
- MIMO 2011 revision (abstract only; full PDF not read): https://pure.rcs.ac.uk/en/publications/the-revision-of-the-hornbostel-sachs-classification-in-2011-by-th/
- Electrophone 51/52/53 (Sachs 1940 wording; the 2011 revised wording NOT verified): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_electrophones_by_Hornbostel%E2%80%93Sachs_number
- Natya Shastra four classes (tata, sushira, avanaddha, ghana): https://swarganga.org/articles/details.php?id=6 and https://www.clearias.com/indian-musical-instruments/
- Bayin eight materials: https://www.britannica.com/art/eight-sound and https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01119234

Coverage check of the other taxonomies: Natya Shastra's four classes map onto chordophone/aerophone/membranophone/idiophone families, so they add no missing category. Bayin: metal/stone (bells, lithophones in tuned percussion), silk (strings), bamboo/gourd/earth (flutes, free reeds, ocarina), skin (drums), wood (woodblocks, drums and percussion) are all covered. The real gaps come from H-S subclasses that no family owns: 12 (plucked idiophones), 13 (friction idiophones), 14 (blown idiophones), 22/23/24 (non-struck membranophones), 41 (free aerophones), mechanical instruments (cross-cutting), site-bound/environmental instruments, and 5x intermediates.

## Proposed new families

### 1. plucked idiophones
Definition: instruments whose sound comes from a rigid tongue (lamella) plucked or twanged and left to vibrate: lamellophones (thumb pianos), jaw harps (guimbardes), music-box combs. Boundary: not struck (tuned percussion keeps xylophone-type, steel tongue drum, tank drums, which are struck 111.x); not free reeds (a jaw harp is currently under free reeds, but it is a plucked idiophone, H-S 121.2, not an air-driven reed); not strings.
H-S: 12 (121.1 clack, 121.21 idioglot and 121.22 heteroglot jaw harps, 122.1/122.2 comb lamellophones).
Examples (all from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamellophone and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew%27s_harp, fetched): mbira, likembe, marimbula, khomus, morsing, dan moi, kubing/kouxian, murchunga. Also music box comb (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphon).
Estimate: 35-50 (many named lamellophone and jaw harp types).

### 2. friction instruments
Definition: idiophones sounded by bowing, rubbing or rotating against a stick, plaque or vessel: musical saw, daxophone, nail violin, glass harp/harmonica, verrophone, Cristal Baschet, musical glasses, bowed glass or metal rods. Boundary: not bowed strings (the vibrating body is solid/glass, not a string); singing bowls count here only when rubbed (struck bowls stay in tuned percussion); friction DRUMS (cuica, lion's roar, H-S 23) stay in drums and percussion by default (see questions).
H-S: 13 (131 sticks, 132 plaques, 133 vessels, 134 sets). Note MIMO revision reclassifies the saw as 151 (per Musical saw page).
Examples: musical saw (132.1, 2-2.5 octaves typical; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_saw), daxophone and nail violin (same page), glass harp/verrophone/terpodion (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_glasses), Cristal Baschet (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristal_Baschet; fetch returned 200, not read in detail: UNVERIFIED H-S number).
Estimate: 15-25.

### 3. noise makers
Definition: free aerophones and signalling/effect sound-makers where the air or the whole object moves rather than being enclosed in a tube: bullroarer, sirens, whirled corrugated tubes, horns and klaxons, whirligigs. Boundary: whistles of 421 (pea whistle, boatswain's call, steam whistle, duck call) are edge-blown and belong in flutes; thunder sheets, ratchets and whips stay in drums and percussion.
H-S: 41 free aerophones (412.21 rotating, 412.22 whirling per the H-S page structure; the Bullroarer page itself gave no number: UNVERIFIED which of the two). Air horns are not simple: diaphragm/reed types versus whistle types (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_horn), so assign by mechanism.
Examples: bullroarer (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullroarer), siren (UNVERIFIED), corrugaphone (UNVERIFIED), warada and buzzer/whirligig (listed under Bullroarer see-also), reed-type air horn/klaxon/martinshorn (UNVERIFIED H-S).
Estimate: 10-15.

### 4. mechanical instruments
Definition: instruments that play themselves from a programmed medium (pinned cylinder, punched disc, paper roll, book): music boxes, disc music boxes (Polyphon), player and reproducing pianos, barrel/fairground/dance organs, orchestrions, serinettes and singing-bird boxes, musical clocks, carillon automata. Boundary: sound production is by existing H-S classes, so this is a cross-cutting category defined by control; it exists because the catalog lacks a place where the automaton is the instrument (timbre of a music box, roll-driven piano). Hand-played instruments with the same sound stay in their own family. Electronic drum machines stay in electronic.
H-S: none of its own (each follows its sound source; e.g. comb music box 122.x, barrel organ 42x).
Examples: Polyphon (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphon), player piano, reproducing piano, orchestrion, panharmonicon, photoplayer, Componium, musical clock (all named at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_piano); barrel organ, fairground organ, dance organ (same page).
Estimate: 20-30. Existing catalog "music box" (tuned percussion) and "calliope" (organs) would move here or be cross-referenced.

### 5. environmental instruments
Definition: site-bound or nature-driven instruments that sound without a player: tide/wave organs, wind-driven instruments, water-driven garden instruments, large architectural installations. Boundary: portable, hand-played versions stay in their family; here only things where the environment (water, wind, building) is a constituent of the instrument.
H-S: spread (aerophone 421 for sea/wave organs; the Wave Organ page calls them hydraulophones/tide organs; 111 for suikinkutsu-type water bells; 31 wind-excited for aeolian harp, UNVERIFIED).
Examples: Wave Organ (San Francisco, 1986), Blackpool High Tide Organ, Sea Organ (Zadar), Comb of the Wind (all at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_Organ); shishi-odoshi (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishi-odoshi; the page says it is garden aural design, NOT clearly a musical instrument); suikinkutsu (not fetched: UNVERIFIED); aeolian harp, singing ringing tree, Great Stalacpipe Organ (not fetched: UNVERIFIED).
Estimate: 10-15. Weakest of the five; see questions.

## Rejected or merged proposals

- friction idiophones: ACCEPTED, renamed `friction instruments` (see 2). Merged waterphone and Cristal Baschet here.
- water idiophones: REJECTED as family (3-6 items: suikinkutsu, waterphone, water-filled bowls). Waterphone goes to friction instruments (bowed rods/ struck bowl, UNVERIFIED), suikinkutsu to environmental instruments. Hydraulophone (H-S "NaN"; fluid-driven, reed or fipple types, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulophone) does not fit any family; park in environmental instruments or flutes (owner question).
- singing membranes / mirlitons: REJECTED as family. H-S 24 is real (241 free kazoos, 242 tube or vessel kazoos) but holds about 5 entries (kazoo, onion flute, eunuch flute, comb-and-paper, zobo). Merge into voices (voice-excited). Swazzle is NOT clearly a mirliton (reed-like; UNVERIFIED), reassign to double reeds or voices (owner question).
- keyboard zithers and fretless/box zithers: REJECTED as families. Mechanisms differ (shahi baaja, bulbul tarang, taishogoto are plucked/struck with keys or levers; marxophone and celestaphone are hammered chord zithers). Merge into plucked strings or struck strings by excitation; dolceola and claviharp go to keyboards. Tiny (about 10) and H-S puts them all in 314.
- ambient/wind-driven + architecture: MERGED into environmental instruments (5).
- found-object (vegetable instruments, car music project): REJECTED. Vegetable orchestra instruments are orchestras/projects; each real instrument already has an H-S slot (flute, etc.). Car music project is not an instrument.
- one-man band / symphonium: REJECTED; performance format, not an instrument (confirm symphonium is a product: UNVERIFIED).
- novelty bowed (yaybahar): REJECTED; yaybahar is a bowed chordophone with spring/membrane resonators, reassign to bowed strings (H-S 321, UNVERIFIED).
- early electrophones and tape keyboards: REJECTED as families. Sachs's 5x split (51 electrically actuated, 52 amplified, 53 radioelectric) leaves all of these in `electronic` (or keyboards for the hand-played tone-source). Telharmonium and mellotron/chamberlin/optigan go to electronic with a replay/electromechanical tag. The 2011 MIMO wording for 51-53 was NOT verified.
- plucked membrane (khamak): REJECTED as family. H-S 22 has no subdivisions on the H-S page; khamak (and gopichand, ektara-like) fits plucked strings.
- noise makers: ACCEPTED but narrowed (whistles to flutes).
- plucked idiophones (jaw harps): ACCEPTED, from the first audit's `plucked idiophones like jaw harps` hint, widened to all of H-S 12.

## Boundary questions for the owner

1. Is a family defined by H-S sound mechanism (idiophone-plucked, friction) or by player/user experience? The 18 existing families mix both (organs vs keyboards, voices vs choirs).
2. Mechanical instruments are cross-cutting: do you want them as their own family (my recommendation) or as a tag on existing ones?
3. Signal whistles, steam whistle, duck call: flutes (mechanism) or noise makers (function)?
4. Friction drums (cuica, lion's roar, H-S 23): drums and percussion (current) or friction instruments?
5. Kazoo/mirliton: voices, or a one-off in noise makers? And swazzle?
6. Electrophones: should electronic be split into electromechanical (51/52: Hammond, Rhodes, Telharmonium) and electronic (53)? The first audit proposes ~45 electronic entries, which would make electronic larger than any other family.
7. Environmental instruments: keep as a family, or drop (the shishi-odoshi page does not call it an instrument)?
8. Sound effects (thunder sheet, wind machine, rain machine, cannon) and vocal techniques (throat singing, beatbox, yodel, ululation): not covered by any family. They are not strictly instruments; include as a `sound effects` family or exclude? I found no authoritative taxonomy for them.
9. "Sections" and articulation variants (violin section, pizzicato, mutes): these are patches, not instruments; not my task, but they inflate the 431.
10. Hydraulophone and other fluid-driven instruments have no H-S slot; which family?

## Bugs/risks noticed
- Existing catalog puts jaw harp under free reeds (wrong H-S class: it is 121.2, plucked idiophone). Same for the first audit's hulusi/bawu inconsistency: hulusi is 422.3 in the audit (a reed in a tube) but listed in free reeds; per the H-S page, free-reed pipes are 422 non-free aerophones with a free reed, so those numbers should be checked.
- First audit's "siren 412.21", "bullroarer 412.22" and "martinshorn 412.132" were NOT verified; 412.132 is "sets of free-reed instruments" per the H-S page, which is a poor fit for a single-note horn.
