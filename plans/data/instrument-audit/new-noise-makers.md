## Summary

- Catalog has 0 entries in this family. Nothing in `catalog_names.txt` moves here (checked: vuvuzela is brass, whip is the slapstick in drums, tin/low whistle are flutes).
- New entries in the TSV: 21 (7 of them were already proposed by the first audit: bullroarer, siren, whirly tube (as corrugaphone), martinshorn, klaxon, air horn, intonarumori; 14 are new finds).
- Proposed entries rejected from this family: 7 (steam whistle, boatswains call, pea whistle, duck call -> other families; kazoo, swazzle -> voices; vuvuzela stays brass).
- Aliases: 22 listed below.
- Range fixes: 8 proposed ranges are unsupported by any source (UNVERIFIED), 3 H-S numbers verified.
- Family size honest estimate: about 20 distinct entries, almost all UNPITCHED or fixed-pitch. Estimate of 10-15 in the family definition is low; 20-21 is supported.
- Bug/risk: first audit's "martinshorn 412.132" is in fact what Wikipedia's H-S list says (412.132 sets of free reeds, alongside accordion, harmonica), so it is VERIFIED against that list even though it is a poor mechanical fit (the siren article says German two-tone horns switch air between high and low air horns; UNVERIFIED whether these have free reeds). The catalog's "whip (slapstick)" is a clapper (concussion idiophone), NOT a whip crack. A whip crack is a distinct H-S 411 displacement free aerophone that nobody proposed.

## Rejected or corrected proposed entries

- steam whistle, boatswains call, pea whistle: whistles are H-S 421.221.11 (Wikipedia list: "Whistle" 421.221.11, slide whistle under 421.221.3); edge-blown, belong in flutes (family definition already says so). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aerophones_by_Hornbostel%E2%80%93Sachs_number . Pea-whistle number 421.221.11 in the proposal is plausible for generic whistle; boatswains call and steam whistle numbers "421.221 (~)" UNVERIFIED.
- duck call: reed call, not a free aerophone; not 41x. Move to double reeds/clarinets (reed type UNVERIFIED); first audit's "422.1 (~)" UNVERIFIED. Not a noise maker by this family's definition.
- kazoo, swazzle: voices (mirliton), per the family decision. Swazzle UNVERIFIED mechanism.
- vuvuzela (already in catalog under brass): Wikipedia says brass instrument, H-S 423.121.22, about 235 Hz (about Bb3), first partial about 465 Hz. VERIFIED: keep in brass; the boundary question is closed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuvuzela
- aerophor (named in brief as siren-adjacent): not an instrument; Bernard Samuels's 1912 foot-operated bellows giving auxiliary breath to wind players. Reject. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerophor
- slide whistle: fipple flute with piston; flutes (also tagged toy instruments). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_whistle
- siren "412.21" and bullroarer "412.22": VERIFIED (see range checks). Note the H-S page's navbox lists 412.21 twice; the body is correct.
- intonarumori in drums_and_percussion_proposed: moved here as in the reassign list, but H-S "112?" is wrong: a wheel-rubbed string is not an idiophone. UNVERIFIED correct number.
- dub siren (electronic proposal): electronic, not here; mention as boundary.
- Boundary notes only (stay elsewhere): ratchet and gragger (112.24, percussion), thunder sheet (111.221+112.1 per Wikipedia, percussion), whip as slapstick (catalog drums), thunder run, rainstick (112.13+133.1, percussion/friction idiophone), wind machine / aeoliphone (Wikipedia says friction idiophone; suggests friction instruments, not here), lion's roar (232 friction drum, drums), udu and boomwhackers and tubulum (413 plosive: percussion/tuned percussion, not noise makers), kazoo/swazzle (voices), whistling kites / pigeon whistles (edge-blown whistles: flutes; Chinese fengzheng bamboo whistles https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-star-malaysia/20120221/285976104090710 ; Met pigeon whistle https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/503905).

## Range checks

- bullroarer 50-300 Hz: UNVERIFIED; the Bullroarer page gives no frequency, only "roaring vibrato". Slat 15-60 cm long, 1.2-5 cm wide VERIFIED (use for the model).
- siren glide G3-G6: UNVERIFIED; article says "several hundred hertz" depending on rotor speed and number of openings. Two-tone mechanical sirens produce a minor third (rotor openings divisible by six and five): VERIFIED. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_(alarm)
- corrugaphone "harmonics of about C3 tube, C4-C6": CORRECTED to harmonics 2-6 (7 with great effort) of whatever tube length; C3 fundamental is UNVERIFIED.
- steam whistle C3-C5, boatswains call C6-C7, pea whistle C6-C7, duck call C4-C5: UNVERIFIED, no source consulted; out of family anyway.
- air horn C4-F4, klaxon A3-D4, martinshorn C4-F4: UNVERIFIED; no source gives pitches. Only verified: vehicle electric horn about 500 Hz and 405-420 Hz.
- vuvuzela: VERIFIED Bb3, about 235 Hz.
- H-S numbers VERIFIED: siren 412.21, bullroarer and whirly tube 412.22, party horn 412.131, martinshorn 412.132, whip and swordblade 411 (list page); vuvuzela 423.121.22.
