## Rejected or corrected proposed entries

- sea organ: a fixed site-specific installation in Zadar, not a playable instrument; no pitch control. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_organ. Reject or move to "sound effects".
- fire organ: duplicate of pyrophone (same thermoacoustic/combustion principle); keep one at most. Not an organ in the H-S sense.
- pyrophone: real (Kastner), but a combustion tube instrument, not an organ-family timbre; move to aerophones or tuned oddities. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrophone
- beer bottle organ, bubble organ, uberorgan, sequential resonation machine: one-off gallery art objects from a single source (oddmusic), no documented range or timbre; reject. (bubble organ is H-S 4 per proposal, i.e. not an aerophone at all; wrong class.)
- telharmonium: real, but it is an early electromechanical (additive, alternator) instrument; no keyboard range stated, and a game would just use the existing tonewheel or additive voice. Wrong family (electronic, not organs). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telharmonium
- choralcelo: real but source gave no timbre; electromagnetic-string/organ hybrid; reject unless a source describes sound. Wrong family (electronic).
- chromelodeon: a retuned reed organ (43-tone). The free-reed boundary: it is H-S 412.132, belongs to free reeds, not organs; its main feature is microtuning, which the catalog lacks; keep only if microtonal tuning is supported.
- regal: keep, valid (Wikipedia Regal (instrument)), but H-S given as 421.222.11 is a guess; a regal is a beating-reed pipe organ, so H-S 422.? (memory). Mark UNVERIFIED.

## Range checks

- regal C3-C6: CORRECTED to C2-C6 (common compass C/E to c''' four octaves, short octave; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regal_(instrument)); Met regal 89.4.3517 spans E to C, 3 1/6 octaves.
- portative: memory guess of two octaves CORRECTED to about one octave plus Bb (9 pipes) per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portative_organ.
- hydraulis: memory claim of 52 pipes in 4 ranks is UNVERIFIED; Wikipedia gives 24 pipes for the Dion instrument; Aquincum count not stated.
- Hammond 61-note manuals VERIFIED; Vox Continental 49-note manuals VERIFIED; Farfisa varies by model.
- Organ stop rows: footage and family VERIFIED, key range UNVERIFIED (organ stops sound at pitch relative to length: 8' = written pitch, 4' an octave up, 16' an octave down, 2 2/3' a twelfth; this is stated in the stops list https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pipe_organ_stops, but the range values I gave are the conventional 61-note manual from memory).
- telharmonium 153 keys: from the cited page, not independently checked; no pitch range.

## Gaps you could not close

- Hydraulis range: need Aquincum reconstruction data (Wikipedia does not give it).
- MIMO and Met have records for regals, barrel organs etc. but the search tool could not open specific MIMO records; none were checked for ranges.
- Kinura, posthorn, diaphonic diapason: not found on Wikipedia stops/theatre pages; need ATOS or builder source.
- Lowrey, Baldwin, Compton Electrone: not looked up.
- Mellotron/Chamberlin, Optigan, Hohner Pianet, Cordovox, Ondioline, Rhodes: belong to electronic/keyboards; confirm they are in those families.
- Harmonium/free-reed organs: Physharmonica, melodeon (reed organ), seraphine, Orgue expressif; boundary with free reeds.
- Cavaille-Coll stops (flute harmonique, gambe), Spanish horizontal reeds (trompeta real, chirimia), Dutch Hoboy, Schalmei, Rankett organ stops; Welte Philharmonic; apollonicon; Wurlitzer Opus; Allen digital organ; Rodgers; digital organs.
- Percussion stops in theatre organs (xylophone, chrysoglott, glockenspiel in organ chests): belong to tuned percussion.
- Tower/Church bell organ: carillon connection.
