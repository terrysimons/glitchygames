# Plucked strings audit (second pass)

Method note: I fetched about 25 Wikipedia pages (List of string instruments, Lute, Zither, Lyre, Banjo, Ukulele, Oud, Gimbri, Qanun, Chapman Stick, Guqin, Geomungo, Valiha, Cavaquinho, Bandurria, Bandura, Gusli, Scheitholt, Diddley bow, Washtub bass, Marovany, Musical bow, Cigar box guitar, Krar, Dan bau). The `List of plucked string instruments` URL is a 404. I did not get to MIMO, the Met or the Horniman. In the new-missing table, `range_status` says VERIFIED only where a fetched page gave open-string tunings or a range. Everything else is UNVERIFIED (memory; estimated from the usual tuning). Likewise, `existence confirmed in fetched Wikipedia list page` means the name appeared in one of those pages. `existence (memory)` means it did not. The source URL is a plausible Wikipedia article title; for the memory rows it was not fetched, so treat it as a lead and not as a citation.

## Summary

- New missing instruments found: 171 (table below; about 63 confirmed in fetched Wikipedia pages, the rest existence from memory).
- Proposed entries rejected or moved out of this family: 14 (marimbula, khamak, marxophone-type items not listed here, harps and keyboard cousins in the proposed list; see section 3).
- Aliases identified: 25.
- Range fixes to proposed entries: 9 corrected, about 12 verified or partly verified, the rest UNVERIFIED.
- Family-placement issues in the catalog: `lyre`, `kora`, `harp guitar` sit in harps (lyre is not a harp; kora is a harp-lute; harp guitar is a guitar with extra open strings); `berimbau` sits in struck strings (it is a struck musical bow, H-S 311.1, fine, but should be cross-referenced with the other musical bows).

Notes on the table:
- Rows for size and register variants (balalaika, domra, ukulele, banjo, tamburica family, bandurria family) are distinct instruments by size and register, not synonyms, per the brief. Several share one synth voice with a transposition.
- `dolceola` and `claviharp` have key actions: the dolceola uses felt hammers, so it is probably struck strings (the Zither page lists it with keyed zithers). Move to struck strings or keyboards if the owner prefers.
- Harp-family items were excluded by instruction (konghou, kora, ngombi, seperewa, bolon, adungu, ennanga, dital harp, harp guitar, clarsach, etc.).

## Rejected or corrected proposed entries

- marimbula: a lamellophone (plucked idiophone), not a chordophone; belongs with the lamellophones/idiophones. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marimbula (memory)
- khamak: a one-string instrument with a membrane resonator (a plucked drum-like chordophone); H-S placement uncertain; the proposal itself notes it may need a new family. Do not add as plucked strings without a decision. (memory)
- krar, begena, nyatiti (proposed under harps): these are lyres, not harps; the Krar page calls it a "five-or-six stringed bowl-shaped lyre". Move into plucked strings. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krar and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre (nyatiti, begena, krar all listed under African lyres)
- sasando (proposed under harps): a tube zither; the Zither page lists it as a zither. Move here. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zither
- swarmandal (proposed under harps): listed as a zither. Move here. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zither
- valiha: a tube zither (verified); accept in this family. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valiha
- marxophone, pianoharp: fretless zithers whose strings are struck by hammers (marxophone) or are keyboard instruments; not plucked. Struck strings or keyboards family. (memory)
- aeolian harp: wind-driven, no performer plucking; not a playable plucked instrument and not a harp. Treat as an oddity or ambient texture, not a plucked-string voice. (memory)
- jouhikko, talharpa: bowed lyres; belong to bowed strings. (memory)
- orphica, tangent piano, spinet, lautenwerck, clavicymbalum: keyboard instruments (some plucked by quills, but family is keyboards). (memory)
- konghou, ngombi, seperewa, gravikord (proposed under harps): harps or harp-lutes; out of scope for this family.
- ngoni: accept but note that it is a spike lute (listed under African lutes on the Lute page), with kamele and donso variants as separate sizes. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lute
- tea chest bass: same instrument class as the washtub bass; the Washtub bass page calls tea chest bass "a variation" and gutbucket the alternate name. Merge. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washtub_bass
- diddley bow: accept; a monochord zither, with no pitch range in the source. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diddley_bow
- musical bow: too generic as a single voice; the Musical bow page describes uhadi, umrhubhe, berimbau etc. as separate types and gives no range, only that every bow has an open and a closed note about a whole tone apart. Keep one generic voice and add specific bows only if wanted. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_bow
- epinette des vosges, langeleik, scheitholt: three related drone zithers; the Scheitholt page says hummel is the northern German name for the scheitholt and gives no relationship to the others. Keep all three if distinct voices are wanted, but they will sound nearly alike. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheitholt
- blaster beam, moodswinger, pikasso guitar, kithara (partch), adapted guitar, fotdella, gittler guitar, pencilina, tritare, nondo, shruti stick (from the oddities audit): real or semi-real experimental instruments, but several are one-off builds with no source for range. The blaster beam is sounded by striking or scraping a long beam, not by plucking. Not verified; low priority for a plucked-string family. (memory)

## Gaps you could not close

- MIMO, the Met and the Horniman were not searched, so museum-only instruments are likely still missing (for example named lyres and lutes from East Africa, Himalayan and Siberian lutes).
- The full Wikipedia `List of string instruments` page was read only through the first 100000 characters, and the Lute, Banjo, Ukulele, Oud and Guqin pages were truncated. Items near the ends of those pages were not seen.
- Ranges for all the new rows other than those marked VERIFIED are estimated from usual tunings from memory and need checking against a source.
- Possible families I suspect but could not confirm: Sami and Siberian lutes, Caucasian plucked zithers (kabardian, abkhaz), Burmese and Thai zithers, Madagascan guitar-types (kabosy), Himalayan lutes (piwang is bowed), more Latin American guitar variants (requinto jarocho, leona, guitarra sexta, tiple requinto), more lute-family names from Europe (mandolute, mandriola, mandotar, guitaro, guitalin, guitarra conchera, mandolina conchera, tremolo bass, angelique and swedish lute appear in the Wikipedia lists but are not in the table). The Wikipedia list names guitarra conchera, guitaro, guitalin, mandolute, mandotar, mandriola, harp lute, swedish lute, sallaneh, chromaharp, nuzhe, khim, yazheng, adjalin, celestaphone, tremoloa, ukelin, violinzither, spitzharfe, shareero, dita, kibugander, endongo, ntongoli, asor, gartner lyre, lirone, lira da braccio, lyra, lijerica, kwitra, sistil, quitra, torban, outi, bipa, sueng, swarabat, tautirut, tambourine de bearn, mexican salterio: these appeared in fetched pages but I did not add them as rows; they are candidates for the next pass.
- Not plucked strings and so out of scope: lira da braccio, lirone, lyra viol, ukelin (bowed or bowed-and-plucked), ajaeng, yazheng (bowed).
- Modeling difficulty summary for the family: sympathetic strings (sitar, sarod, rudra veena, mohan veena, ten-string guitar, Chilean guitarron, Portuguese guitar, hardanger-like) are not available; buzz bridges (sitar jawari, begena, nyatiti, gimbri rattles, electric sitar) need a nonlinear waveshaper; slide and bend instruments (dan bau, ektara, steel guitars, vichitra veena, vong co guitar, mohan veena, washtub bass) need a pitch-glide envelope; mouth bows (ukeke, umrhubhe) need a moving formant filter; tapped instruments (Chapman stick, warr guitar, harpejji) need two simultaneous voices; qanun needs microtonal level selection.
